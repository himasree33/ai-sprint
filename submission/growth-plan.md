# AI Sprint — Growth Plan (5-slide outline)

> Simulation for the NxtWave Growth Intern Round 1 Challenge. All numbers are **planning assumptions to validate**, not results.
> Goal: 500 final-year engineering students register for the free online workshop *Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes* within 7 days, with ₹2,000.

---

## Slide 1 — Target audience & student insight

- **Audience:** Final-year engineering students in India who want practical AI skills, portfolio projects, and more confidence discussing projects in internship and placement interviews.
- **Insight (hypothesis to validate):** Some students may be interested in AI but hesitate because they think they need advanced coding skills, expensive tools, or weeks of preparation.
- **Implication:** Lead with *“what could I build?”* rather than *“attend a workshop”*. Give value before asking for registration.
- **How we'd validate:** Experiment 1 (project-focused vs generic copy) + objections reported by ambassadors.

## Slide 2 — Growth strategy & prioritised channels

| # | Channel | Target | Why under ₹2,000 |
|---|---------|-------:|------------------|
| 1 | College ambassadors & clubs | 220 | Trusted peers; cost = small, performance-linked incentives |
| 2 | WhatsApp referrals | 150 | Every registrant becomes a distributor; free and compounding |
| 3 | LinkedIn & Instagram organic | 80 | Project demos build credibility; no paid boost by default |
| 4 | Direct student-community outreach | 50 | Coding clubs / Discords — costs time, not money |

Core asset: **AI Sprint** — an interactive project-idea generator → registration → referral link → WhatsApp share loop.

**Budget:** Ambassador incentives ₹1,000 · Creative assets ₹300 · Contingency / targeted distribution ₹700 (released after Day 4 to the most efficient channel only). Free distribution gets priority because it scales with effort, not money.

## Slide 4 — 500-registration model & three experiments

- **Model:** 220 + 150 + 80 + 50 = **500**. Sanity checks: ≈11 active ambassadors × 20 registrations; 150 referrals ≈ 0.43 referred registrations per non-referred registrant (150 ÷ 350).
- **Attribution rule:** one primary channel per registration; referral attribution tracked separately to avoid double-counting.
- **Experiments (proposed):**
  1. *Message positioning* — project-focused vs generic invite → registrations ÷ tracked visits.
  2. *Referral activation* — prominent share prompt vs standard confirmation → referred registrations per eligible registrant.
  3. *Channel efficiency* — compare registrations, spend and effort by channel → cost/registration, registrations per hour.
